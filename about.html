Why Is It Called "PhonbireWorks"?


A couple of years ago, I decided to come up with a "business name", and at first, I had no idea what to call it. So I went to a random name generator, and found the name "Fonbire". I found this name interesting, however, I was not happy with the spelling. So I changed it up a little, just to give the name some more elegance. I ended up with "Phonbire Art". At this time, I was still into doing artsy things, like digital painting. Fast forward to recent times, I changed my name from "Phonbire Art", to "PhonbireWorks", as this is a more... "general" name that isn't tied to just art. You may have guessed that this name is a play on the word "bonfire", which explains the orange colour scheme I've chosen for this website. I find it neat. The name sounds warm.


That's Cool, But Why The 90's Style Website?


Websites today are designed in a way which is supposed to look "good" on all device screens. It's usually good practice to follow standard modern web design rules. We call this "responsive web design". While it may look minimalist, and sleek, it comes at the cost of user experience. Modern websites are notorious for having oversized text, images, slow loading times, annoying animations, but also hiding useful information inside places like hamburger menus (☰) and drop-down menus, making it feel claustrophobic. Old websites made using pixel-perfect layouts were better at getting straight to the point, showing the user what they want, without them hunting through menus, animations, and oversized media. Not all modern websites are badly made though. But the reason why I went for a pixel-perfect website layout, is that it is much simpler to customize and maintain. I'd rather not put most of my attention in responsive website design, and just focus on my work. I actually used to have a responsive website, but I quickly found it got bloated with tonnes of lines of markup code, as well as JavaScript. This current website uses very minimal amounts of CSS, and is majority HTML. I might return to having a responsive website, but I am enjoying the simplicity of this one. The benefit of a simplistic website like this is it's loading time. People with slow internet connections can use a less bloated website without all the modern fluff like WordPress PHP plugins, animations, unpotimized images, etc.
